## Target-Pathway Mapping

### Which pathways does each target control?

| Target | Primary Pathway | Upstream Signals | Downstream Effects | RA Cell Types Affected |
|--------|----------------|------------------|--------------------|-----------------------|
| **TYK2** | JAK-STAT (IFN, IL-12, IL-23) | Viral/danger signals → DC activation | Th1/Th17 differentiation, IFN signature | DCs, NK, T cells |
| **BTK** | B-cell receptor, FcγR, FcεR | Antigen → BCR crosslinking | B-cell activation, Ab production, macrophage/mast cell activation | B cells, macrophages, mast cells |
| **JAK2** | JAK-STAT (IL-6, GM-CSF, IFN-γ) | Cytokine receptor ligation | M1 polarization, myelopoiesis, acute phase response | Macrophages, myeloid progenitors |
| **IRAK4** | TLR/IL-1R (MyD88-dependent) | PAMPs/DAMPs → TLR activation | NF-κB activation, cytokine production | Macrophages, DCs |
| **PTPN22** | TCR/BCR signaling threshold | Antigen recognition | Lowers activation threshold → autoimmunity | T cells, B cells |

## The IFN Signature in RA — TYK2 Relevance

Approximately 40-50% of RA patients have an elevated type I interferon (IFN) gene signature in blood and synovium:

### IFN Signature Genes (ISGs) Elevated in RA

| Gene | Function | Fold Change in IFN-high RA |
|------|----------|---------------------------|
| MX1 | Antiviral effector | +4.2x |
| IFIT1 | Antiviral effector | +3.8x |
| IFI44L | IFN-induced protein | +3.5x |
| ISG15 | Ubiquitin-like modifier | +3.2x |
| OAS1 | Antiviral enzyme | +2.9x |
| RSAD2 | Antiviral effector | +2.7x |
| SIGLEC1 | Monocyte activation marker | +2.5x |

**Clinical significance**:
- Patients with high IFN signatures respond POORLY to TNF inhibitors
- Patients with high IFN signatures may preferentially respond to TYK2 inhibition
- The IFN signature could serve as a patient selection biomarker for TYK2 therapy

## The B-Cell Contribution — BTK Relevance

### Autoantibodies in RA

| Autoantibody | Target | Prevalence | Pathogenic Role |
|--------------|--------|------------|-----------------|
| Rheumatoid Factor (RF) | IgG Fc region | 70-80% | Immune complex formation |
| Anti-CCP (ACPA) | Citrullinated proteins | 60-70% | Osteoclast activation, NETosis |
| Anti-carbamylated protein | Carbamylated proteins | 30-40% | Complement activation |

**BTK's role**: All three autoantibody classes depend on B-cell activation through BCR signaling (BTK-dependent). Additionally:
- Immune complexes activate macrophages via FcγR (BTK-dependent)
- This creates a feed-forward loop: B cells → autoantibodies → immune complexes → macrophage activation → more inflammation → more B-cell activation

### Tertiary Lymphoid Structures (TLS) in RA Synovium

- 40% of RA synovia contain organized B-cell follicles (TLS)
- TLS presence correlates with disease severity and erosion
- B cells in TLS undergo local class switching and affinity maturation
- BTK inhibition may be particularly effective in TLS-rich patients

## The Combination Rationale: Why Single Targets May Not Suffice

**Problem**: RA is a heterogeneous disease with multiple parallel pathogenic mechanisms. Single-target approaches leave other mechanisms active.

**Evidence for multi-mechanism disease**:
1. TNF inhibitors — 60% response, but only 30% achieve remission
2. Rituximab (B-cell depletion) — works, but T-cell and macrophage inflammation persists
3. JAK inhibitors (pan-JAK) — broad efficacy, but safety limits dosing
4. Single-target BTK — modest efficacy in all-comers

**Proposed rational combinations**:

| Combination | Mechanisms Covered | Predicted Efficacy | Safety Consideration |
|-------------|-------------------|-------------------|---------------------|
| TYK2 + BTK | IFN/Th1/Th17 + B-cell/macrophage/mast cell | High (>80% of RA mechanisms) | Low overlap in toxicity |
| TYK2 + anti-TNF | IFN/Th1/Th17 + direct TNF blockade | High | Infection risk (additive) |
| BTK + JAK1 | B-cell + broad cytokine | Very high | Immunosuppression concern |

**The TYK2 + BTK combination is theoretically optimal** because:
- Non-overlapping mechanisms (IFN axis vs. B-cell axis)
- Non-overlapping toxicity profiles
- Both targets are elevated in early disease
- Both are druggable kinases with oral small molecules available
- Both have blood-based biomarkers for PD monitoring

---

## Conclusions

1. RA is a multi-mechanism disease requiring multi-target approaches for optimal outcomes
2. The IFN signature (TYK2-dependent) and B-cell/autoantibody axis (BTK-dependent) represent two major orthogonal pathogenic pathways
3. Patient stratification by IFN signature and serostatus can guide target selection
4. The TYK2 + BTK combination addresses the broadest range of RA pathology with a favorable safety profile
5. Novel targets (IRAK4, PTPN22) may complement but are earlier in development
